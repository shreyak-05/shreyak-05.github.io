Attention is All You Need — and Why It Matters for Robotics

2025 Shreya Kalyanaraman ~8 min read Vaswani et al., 2017

What This Paper Actually Did

Before transformers, sequence modeling was dominated by RNNs and CNNs — models that processed information sequentially or locally. Vaswani et al. proposed something radical: throw all of that out, and build a model entirely from attention mechanisms. No recurrence. No convolution.

The result was faster to train (parallelizable), better at capturing long-range dependencies, and — as it turned out — surprisingly general across modalities.

The Architecture

The transformer is built from an encoder-decoder structure. Each is composed of 6 identical layers. The encoder processes the input sequence; the decoder generates the output autoregressively.

Encoder Layer (×6)
Self-Attention — mixes information across all token positions
Feed-Forward — transforms representations non-linearly
↳ both wrapped with residual connections + layer norm
Decoder Layer (×6)
Masked Self-Attention — attends only to past positions (causal)
Cross-Attention — attends over encoder output
Feed-Forward

Scaled Dot-Product Attention

The core operation: given queries Q, keys K, and values V, compute softmax(QKᵀ / √d_k) · V. The scaling by √d_k prevents the dot products from growing large in high dimensions, which would push softmax into regions with very small gradients.

Multi-head attention runs this in parallel across multiple representation subspaces, letting the model attend to information from different positions and with different "lenses" simultaneously.

Positional Encoding

Attention is permutation-invariant — it has no notion of order. Positional encodings inject sequence order by adding sinusoidal signals to the token embeddings. The choice of sinusoids is deliberate: they allow the model to generalize to sequence lengths unseen during training.

Why I Care About This for Robotics

The most important property of the transformer for embodied AI is that it's modality-agnostic. Attention treats all inputs as sequences of tokens. It doesn't care if those tokens come from images, language, or action trajectories — as long as you can tokenize it, attention can mix it.

This is directly what gave birth to the VLA models I work with:

  • RT-2 — web-pretrained vision-language model fine-tuned to output robot actions as tokens
  • π₀ — diffusion policy built on a transformer backbone, trained across multiple robot embodiments
  • OpenVLA — open-source VLA using SigLIP + LLaMA 2, mapping image+instruction → discretized actions

Cross-attention is what makes multimodal grounding possible — language instructions can attend over visual tokens, and action predictions can attend over both. The architecture scales.

An Open Problem Worth Thinking About

Here's where it gets interesting. Cross-attention enables alignment across modalities, but how reliably does it actually do that for embodied tasks?

Text and images have obvious semantic overlap — a model trained on web data sees "cat" and photos of cats together constantly. But "move the wrist 3 degrees while maintaining contact force below 8N" has no natural web-data correlate. The grounding is brittle at the edges.

Open Question
While extensions exist — sensor tokens, trajectory models, contact-aware attention — their reliability for safe execution of contact-rich manipulation tasks remains limited. This points to a real gap: transformers built for language aren't inherently equipped for physical interaction, and that's where architectural innovation still has room to run.

My work on the TMI project is partially motivated by this: even in the linguistic domain, OpenVLA is brittle to surface-form variation that humans find trivial. If the language grounding is already shaky, the action grounding downstream is even harder to make robust.

Strengths and Weaknesses

What makes this paper great

  • Demonstrated that attention alone — without recurrence — can outperform RNN-based models on machine translation, opening the door to scale
  • The modality-agnostic framing turned out to be the most important thing. The authors may not have fully anticipated it, but the architecture's generality is why it took over NLP, vision, and now robotics
  • Positional encodings are an elegant hack that still hold up — simple, interpretable, and they work

Limitations

  • Quadratic attention complexity in sequence length — FlashAttention and its successors address this, but the fundamental bottleneck remains
  • No inductive bias toward physical structure — for robotics, this matters more than for text
  • Positional encodings feel like a workaround. Rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) are a better solution for long contexts
